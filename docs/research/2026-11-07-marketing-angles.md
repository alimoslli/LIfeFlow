# Marketing angles for one 20-second video and one image post (students 18 to 24)

Date: 2026-11-07. Author: market researcher. For: the owner (CEO), then Product & Design and Marketing.

## 1. Question

Which hook, pain and real feature should LifeFlow's first short video (20 seconds, 9:16) and first image post lead with, for college students 18 to 24?

## 2. Short answer

The hard evidence for "what hooks work right now" is thin. I could not open TikTok or Instagram, and the pages I found are marketing-agency blogs, so I treat their advice as direction only. What is solid is the pain: working students describe the same squeeze (classes, a job, money, almost no sleep), and my earlier reports found no app that joins class schedule, shifts and money. The honest gap LifeFlow can own is "the dots between your classes, your shifts and your bank balance", shown with a real alert that arrives before the student asks. I recommend the **shift vs exam clash** angle for the 20-second video and the **named bill before payday** angle for the image post.

## 4. Findings

### F1. Hook formats: what the advice says (weak evidence, vendor blogs)

- Most sources say the hook has to land in about 2 to 3 seconds; one says about 1.5 seconds on TikTok. They disagree on the number, so treat it as "very fast".
- POV framing is praised for app ads because it looks native and makes the viewer feel they already use the product. One guide suggests no text for the first two seconds and putting the app screen in at roughly seconds 3 to 10.
- Pain-point openers, "I tested X" and specific outcomes are listed as recurring winners. One guide warns that opening with the app name or logo is a common mistake. Another says to test at least two hook lines per idea.
- The numbers in these articles (retention gains and so on) come from the vendors themselves. I am not repeating them.
- Student-culture side: a Fordham student paper describes a #LockIn trend (focus, productivity) and an #AcademicValidation trend, and students are split, with some calling it encouragement and others calling it unhealthy comparison. A Daily Dot piece calls Notion life-organizing a student trend with a "studyblr/StudyTok" look. Both are older than "right now" and neither is about an app like ours.
- **LifeFlow mapping:** the existing Reel 1 script already follows the advice (hook in the first 2 seconds, app screen by second 3). Its opener "I didn't open this app for 7 days" is a pain-and-outcome hook, not a logo. For 20 seconds, cut it down to one beat, not seven. Test two hook lines (the playbook already lists five alternates).
- **Evidence grade:** weak. Gap: no real student-app examples with links. See section 9.

### F2. What competitors say (very little found)

- **Cleo:** a Shorty Awards entry describes a "Money Main Character" campaign aimed at Gen Z money anxiety and shame, with a "we're in this together" message, and the company's "chief spending officer" posting on TikTok and asking followers to vote on her spending decisions. The page gave no date, so I cannot say it is from 2026. Cleo also sells credit and cash advances, and my earlier report (2026-11-05 students) records an FTC case against it. Its tone is friendly and jokey.
- **Motion, Reclaim, Notion Calendar:** only comparison articles. One comparison reports Motion as the most aggressive AI day planner at $19 per seat per month billed annually, and Reclaim as gentler (free tier, $10 per seat paid), with Notion Calendar free. These are business-calendar tools, priced for work teams. I did not find a student campaign.
- **Rocket Money, Copilot, Monarch:** reviewers frame them as subscription cutting (Rocket Money), design-led and iPhone-only (Copilot), and couples or households (Monarch). None is framed for a working student with a class schedule.
- **The gap (from this and from the earlier reports):** a search for an app that combines class schedule, work shifts and budget turned up none, and a second search today said the same: organizer apps and money apps are separate, and gig apps help earn, not plan. This is a finding about search results, not proof that no such app exists, so ads must not say "nobody else does this" (playbook rule: never claim others can't).
- **LifeFlow mapping:** supports vision section 2 (every piece of life is a variable). The honest line is "built around connecting these", not "the only one".

### F3. Real student pains (in their words, paraphrased; sources in section 8)

Counts: this is a read of a handful of threads and pages, not a review tally. I did not count reviews for this report, so I give no percentages.

1. **Work plus a full course load leaves no room.** An r/college mirror says with a heavy credit load and 30 hours a week you can expect "10+ hours of work a day". (Reused from the 2026-11-05 students report.)
2. **Sleep is what goes.** A full-time student on one job says they get 3 to 4 hours of sleep, bed at 2 am up at 5:30, with "chronic exhaustion" (r/ mirror, June 2026, reused).
3. **Work and an exam collide and the boss does not move.** A recent roundup of Reddit stories (search summary, 2026) includes a nursing student who goes from clinical straight to a 7 pm to 7 am shift and whose manager would not adjust the schedule, and a student who quit after being kept past her shift and nearly missing an exam. I could not open the originals, so I use these only as a pointer.
4. **Planners die from upkeep.** Notion's own student story: "I didn't have time to upkeep a detailed template. I ended up abandoning the system altogether" (reused).
5. **Forgetting.** An App Store reviewer of a student planner says they forget assignments and tests. A student blog says a paper planner is left at home when something new is assigned (search summaries; originals not opened).
6. **Money is lumpy.** Students on hourly pay and aid refunds cannot predict their month (reused from earlier reports, with the rule that we never count pay before it lands).
- **LifeFlow mapping:** pains 1 to 4 and 6 are the vision's core (time, work, money, collisions between them). Pain 4 tells us the video must never show setup or logging.

## 5. Things to avoid

Carried over from the three earlier reports (the ones that matter for ads), plus new ones from today.

From the earlier reports:
1. **No guilt or pressure words in the video or caption.** No "you missed", "slipping", "you haven't", no streak talk (notifications report, rules 1 and 15).
2. **No lazy-app picture.** Planners die from upkeep, so never show typing in tasks or logging money. Show the app doing it from connections (students report, rule 1; notifications rule 11).
3. **Never show expected or hourly pay as money you have, and never quote a payday that is not confirmed.** The payday split alert only shows after pay lands (students rule 2; notifications rule 13).
4. **Name the charge and the day.** A flat "your balance is low" is weak. Warn about a named bill on a named day (notifications rule 3). The playbook's "Checking could go below $0 around Friday" must be paired with the named bill in the same shot.
5. **Do not copy what Canvas already sends.** Show a clash (a due date meeting a shift), not a plain due-date reminder (notifications rule 4).
6. **Never suggest taking sleep, cash advances or moving money.** The shift-vs-exam fix is a study block placed in free time, not "sleep less" (students rules 6 and 9).
7. **Do not quote weak numbers** (students rule 10).
8. **No price or trial claims.** The playbook says free while in testing and paid at launch; the owner's price is not public. Say nothing about price, and no App Store badge until live.

New from this research:
9. **Do not say or imply that other assistants or apps cannot connect to things.** Say LifeFlow is built around it (playbook truth rule).
10. **Do not open with the logo or the app name.** Hook with the problem or the outcome (F1; vendor advice, weak evidence, but cheap to follow).
11. **Do not copy Cleo's tone or its creator format.** Cleo's friend-who-jokes voice is theirs, and a cash-advance company's brand story is not ours. Our voice is calm, dry, short (playbook).
12. **Do not use the grind-culture frame** (#LockIn, "that girl" hustle). Students are split on it and some say it breeds comparison (Fordham piece). LifeFlow's promise is "fits your real week", not "do more".
13. **Do not present the animated character as the founder.** The founder line ("built by a student who works 30 hours a week") is a real claim; the character is a made-up person. Say it in the caption or voiceover, not by implying the cartoon is him.
14. **Do not put real private data on screen.** Demo account only, and the alert must be a real screen recording of the app (playbook).
15. **Never use the name "Jarvis".** Say "your personal assistant".

## 6. Situation seeds

- **Student with a retail job:** shift posted for Wednesday night, bio exam Thursday morning. The assistant spots the clash and offers a study block before the shift.
- **Student with an hourly job:** phone bill due Friday, paycheck not in yet. The assistant names the bill and the day, and offers a reminder.
- **Student in clinicals plus night shifts:** clinical ends at 3 pm, a 7 pm shift follows. The assistant keeps the gap between them free and never places study in sleep time.
- **Student who quit planners:** built a template in week one, dropped it by week three. The assistant shows the week from the Canvas feed with no setup.

## 7. 3 angle proposals

### Angle 1: "The shift and the exam" (RECOMMENDED FOR THE 20-SECOND VIDEO)

- **Hook (one line):** "My boss gave me Wednesday night. My bio exam is Thursday. My phone caught it before I did."
- **Pain it hits:** work and school colliding with no warning; the 10+ hour days and thin sleep (F3 items 1 to 3).
- **Real feature it shows:** the schedule clash alert ("Bio exam Thursday overlaps your shift Wednesday") with the **Block it** button that adds study time. Optionally one closing beat: the morning brief the next day. Both are in PLAYBOOK section 2.
- **Why it should work:** it is the single sharpest picture of the thing no other app was found to join (classes plus shifts), so it is our honest gap (F2). The pain is the most repeated in student threads (F3). It follows the vendor advice for pain-point POV with the app screen by second 3 (F1, weak evidence), and it needs no logging (avoid 2). It is also one beat, which fits 20 seconds (hook 0 to 3 s, alert 3 to 9 s, tap Block it 9 to 14 s, study block appears and line "it was already on it" 14 to 18 s, logo 18 to 20 s).
- **Check before filming:** the demo account must really produce this clash alert, and the shift and exam on screen must be the demo data (playbook video log already flags this).
- **Size:** small (reuses Reel 1's Tuesday beat).
- **What it proves:** whether the "connected classes plus work" story stops the scroll more than the generic "0 opens" story.

### Angle 2: "The bill before payday" (RECOMMENDED FOR THE SINGLE IMAGE POST)

- **Hook (one line, on the image):** "Phone bill, $45, due Friday. Checking won't cover it. Your assistant told you Wednesday."
- **Pain it hits:** lumpy student money and surprise overdrafts (F3 item 6).
- **Real feature it shows:** a real screenshot of the bill-due alert with the **Remind me** button, together with the low-balance warning (both in PLAYBOOK section 2). It names the bill and the day, as the notifications report asks.
- **Why it should work:** a still image lets the viewer read one concrete alert in two seconds, which suits a static post better than a moving story. It is plain and a little dry, in our voice, and it avoids the Cleo-style comedy. Evidence for the pain is from earlier reports (hourly pay, aid refunds) and is not new today; I found no engagement data for image posts, so the choice rests on format fit, not proof.
- **Check before posting:** the amount and day must match the demo data. Do not add "payday" copy unless pay has landed in the demo data (never count pay early). No price, no store badge.
- **Size:** small.
- **What it proves:** whether money-first or school-first draws more saves and shares, since the video is school-first.

### Angle 3: "Built by a student who works 30 hours a week" (hold for a later video)

- **Hook (one line):** "Seven classes. Thirty hours a week at work. No app connected any of it, so I built one."
- **Pain it hits:** the whole squeeze, and the feeling that app makers do not live it (F3 items 1, 2, 4).
- **Real feature it shows:** the Today page (the whole day at a glance) and a two-second view of Canvas syncing, then one alert.
- **Why it should work:** the founder story is the brand's strongest honest point (playbook section 1) and a real person to camera is the only format in this report that Cleo-style brand accounts also use (a human, not a logo). But it needs the owner on camera, and I have no engagement evidence for it. It also risks the "founder talks about himself" feel with a cartoon character in the same series, so it should be its own post.
- **Size:** medium (owner films).
- **What it proves:** whether the person matters more than the feature.

### Recommendation

- **20-second video: Angle 1.** One clear collision, one tap, no setup.
- **Image post: Angle 2.** One readable alert, a different pain, so the two posts together show school and money joined.
- They are different on purpose, so the first two posts also test which side people respond to.
