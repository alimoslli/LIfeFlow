# Top ads reference board: what the best assistant and student ads do

Date: 2026-11-07. Author: market researcher. For: the owner (CEO), then Marketing.
Board files (internal, not in the repo): `/tmp/claude-0/-workspace-LIfeFlow/f88947bb-17ba-59ee-934a-43df1518cad1/scratchpad/refs/` (`board.json`, 14 cards, `notes.md`).

## 1. Question

Which real, high-performing short ads and brand films should LifeFlow model for an ad people keep watching, and what should we never copy?

## 2. Short answer

The assistant brands with the biggest budgets keep making the same film: a real person uses the assistant to show up for someone they love, shot like a movie, in their own voice (Google "Dream Job", "New Home", "Loretta"; OpenAI's 30 s spots on 35mm; Anthropic's "Keep thinking" on film). That is the CEO's chosen direction, and the roommate birthday story fits it closely. OpenAI's "Road Trip" (a brother plans a surprise for his sister, 30 s) is the best template for the first spot. The two dangers are both on record: Google's "Dear Sydney" was pulled because the AI wrote a child's personal letter, and "Loretta" moved half its viewers but made some call it creepy. I could not open TikTok's Top Ads, Reddit, video frames or any thumbnails, so numbers here are YouTube page counts and craft details come from press summaries.

## 4. Findings

### F1. The winning shape for assistant ads is "help me show up for someone I love" (5 of 13 references)

- **Evidence:** Google "Dream Job" (Super Bowl 2025): 44,878,857 views, 12,511 likes on the watch page. A dad rehearses a job interview with Gemini Live and draws on raising his daughter; it ends at her college drop-off (Lurzer's Archive, TODAY via search). Google "New Home" (Super Bowl 2026): 100,333,715 views on the search page; a mother and son picture their new home together; Kellogg ranked it the top Super Bowl ad of 2026 (search summary). Google "Loretta" (2020): a widower asks the Assistant to remember his late wife; Unruly measured 49% of viewers with an intense emotional response and ranked it the most effective ad of that game (search summary).
- **Conclusion:** the assistant is a helper and the payoff is for another person. The roommate story (she forgot; the app did not forget her; her friend's birthday still happens) is the same shape.
- **LifeFlow mapping:** supports "solve, don't just warn" and the vision's line through time: the ad can show the app protecting an evening days ahead.

### F2. Film texture is now the trust signal for AI brands (2 brands said it out loud)

- **Evidence:** Anthropic's "Keep thinking" was shot entirely on physical film for "a raw, human texture" rather than the look of AI tech ads (search summary of trade press). OpenAI's first ChatGPT campaign was shot on 35mm by Miles Jay, with custom lenses for warmth; OpenAI's CMO said the process is "very intentionally anchored in human creativity" (search summaries of Creative Review, It's Nice That).
- **Conclusion:** the CEO's rejection of generic AI-looking characters and sets matches what the market leaders do. AI-looking footage in an assistant ad argues against the product.

### F3. 30 seconds is enough when the stake is small and social (OpenAI series)

- **Evidence:** OpenAI's 30 s spots on the search page: "Dish" 187,351 views, "Road Trip" 104,639, "Reddys vs Retirement" 25,763, "Suraj vs The Future" 22,292. "Dish" (cooking for a date) is about 2.9x the median of the four; "Road Trip" (surprising a sibling) about 1.6x.
- **Conclusion:** the two with another person at stake did best. Weak evidence (four spots, paid views), but it agrees with F1.

### F4. Dialogue without fake faces: four working answers

- **Evidence:** the person's own voice to the phone (R01, R03); a human actor playing the assistant (Anthropic Super Bowl spots, real actors in Los Angeles, search summary); talking objects (Google #BestPhonesForever "Lifesaver", 46,662,571 views); the product's screen as the punchline (Spotify India "How To Get Over Your Ex", 8,591,534 views, a friend grabs a phone and reads the Wrapped).
- **LifeFlow mapping:** answers the CEO's rejection of AI on-camera dialogue. For the roommate story the best fit is no lip-synced lines at all: voice-over or the sound of the room, with the friend reading the real screen (demo account).

### F5. The two public failures are exactly our red lines

- **Evidence:** "Dear Sydney" (444,027 views) was pulled from the Olympics rotation after critics said the AI replaced a child's own words; Google said it "tested well before airing" (Hollywood Reporter quote via search). "Loretta" drew "some in tears, others creeped out" (NPR headline). Anthropic's Super Bowl jab at a rival drew "clearly dishonest" from that rival's CEO and critics called it a miss (search summaries).
- **LifeFlow mapping:** matches the playbook (never claim others cannot do things) and the earlier reports' rules.

## 5. Proposals (ranked)

1. **Make the roommate spot on the "Road Trip" shape (R06), 30 s.** One friend, a surprise for another, the app as a quiet helper, real actors, warm film grade, no lip-synced lines. Why: F1, F3, F2. Size: medium (a real shoot or a real creator, not AI faces). Proves: whether story beats product demo on watch time and shares.
2. **End on the friend reading the real screen (R09 move).** The roommate leans over, sees the app already moved the shift swap note and protected Saturday, and wants it. Real screen recording, demo account. Why: F4. Size: small inside the shoot. Proves: whether "the friend wants it" lands as the call to action.
3. **Keep one warm "remember" line, told by her, not known by the app (R03, done carefully).** Why: F1 and F5. Size: small. Proves: whether "the app didn't forget her" feels caring and not creepy (test with five students before posting).
4. **Later: a real working-student creator using LifeFlow in their usual format, marked as an ad (direction D).** Why: R12 at our scale. Size: medium. Proves: creator reach versus brand films.

## 6. Things to avoid (rules)

1. **The app never does the personal part.** No writing the birthday message, the card or the toast, no picking the gift (Dear Sydney).
2. **No "it knows things she never told it."** Show where the knowledge came from (her calendar, her shifts, something she said) so "remember" is caring, not creepy (Loretta's split).
3. **No AI-looking people or sets, no AI lip sync.** Film texture, real rooms, real actors or a real creator (CEO's rule, F2).
4. **No glorified stress or all-nighters.** "Stress is fuel" (Apple 2026 college ad, per Ad Age's headline) is the grind frame we avoid.
5. **No guilt-driven reminders or nagging-mascot humor** (Duolingo's tone; playbook).
6. **No jabs at rivals** (BestPhonesForever, Claude Super Bowl; playbook).
7. **Do not treat big view counts as proof of love.** Brand ad views are bought.
8. **The app's turn must be a real screen recording of a real feature** (playbook truth rules). No edited notifications.
9. **Never show pay before it lands, and no "sleep less" fix** in the shift story (earlier reports).

## 7. Situation seeds

- **Student with a part-time job, dorm roommate:** extra shifts posted the week of her roommate's birthday; the assistant notices the clash with the dinner they planned and offers a swap request draft for her to send, and a study block moved to earlier in the week.
- **Student with a part-time job:** she said once in passing that her friend's birthday is the 14th; the assistant brings it up three days before with the free evening it found, and stays quiet about what to buy.
